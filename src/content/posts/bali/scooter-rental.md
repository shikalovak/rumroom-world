## Hook

Without a scooter, Bali feels small. You're trapped in your neighborhood while better surf spots, hidden cafes, and those Instagram-worthy sunsets all sit "somewhere across town." Taxis drain your wallet faster than you'd expect. The truth: almost every traveler here rents a bike within days. It's not just transportation—it's the key to actually experiencing the island. A scooter costs less than a fancy breakfast and opens up your entire stay.

## Quick hits

- **Daily rental:** $5–$10 for basic models (Honda Scoopy, Vario); $6–$15 for mid-range
- **Monthly:** $50–$120 (Scoopy), up to $225+ for premium bikes
- **You need:** passport for rental; International Driving Permit + home license for legal riding
- **Honestly?** Offline rentals beat online—haggle for 10–15% off if you stay a week+
- **Insurance:** Skip it for budget bikes; grab it if you're renting an NMAX or nervous
- **Police checks:** Carry your IDP. If stopped without one, expect a $15–$30 on-the-spot settlement
- **Left-hand traffic** takes 2–3 days to stop feeling weird

> 💱 *All prices in USD. EU readers: 1 USD ≈ €0.92.*

## How much does a scooter cost in Bali?

Prices shift by length of rental, model, and neighborhood. Here's what you'll actually pay:

| Rental Period | Budget (Scoopy/Beat) | Mid-Range (Vario/PCX) | Premium (NMAX/XMAX) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Daily | $5–$7 | $6–$10 | $15–$25 |
| Weekly | $30–$45 | $40–$60 | $90–$150 |
| Monthly | $50–$75 | $120–$170 | $225–$360 |
| 6+ months | Discounted + security deposit $60–$120 | Discounted + deposit | Discounted + deposit |

**Real talk:** the sweet spot is weekly or monthly. Daily rentals run expensive; anything beyond a month usually gets you a better rate and deposit structure.

## Where to rent

### Offline vs. Online

**Offline** wins on price. You walk into a rental shop, haggle face-to-face, and often knock 10–15% off the menu price. Downside: you're stuck in whatever neighborhood has shops. Canggu has dozens; Ubud too.

**Online** (Rentalmotor, Turo, local shop websites) handles delivery to your hotel. Convenient, but expect 15–20% markup over street rates. Use it if you're lazy or arriving late.

### By neighborhood

| Area | Vibe | Price Level | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Canggu** | Touristy, lots of traffic | Higher | €1.3M+ IDR/month for Scoopy (that's ~$80+) |
| **Ubud** | Quiet, hillier roads | Higher | Same mark-up as Canggu; fewer options |
| **Seminyak/Kuta** | Balanced, close to airport | Medium | $60–$100/month for Scoopy |
| **Uluwatu** | Coastal, cliffside | Medium-High | Mid-range between Canggu and Sanur |
| **Sanur/Denpasar** | Local, honest prices | Lowest | $55–$70/month for Scoopy; real deals here |

**Pro move:** If you're staying in Canggu but don't need the bike immediately, take a bemo (minibus) to Sanur for 30 minutes and rent there. Saves $15–$25 per month, easy.

## How to bargain

Local secret, don't tell—rental shop owners expect negotiation. Here's what works:

1. **Book longer.** "I want a month" sounds better than "five days." Monthly rates are already discounted; they'll negotiate harder to lock you in.
2. **Compare prices out loud.** "The shop next door quoted me 1M IDR." Often shaves off 100–200k ($6–$12).
3. **Smile. Be chill.** Overly aggressive bargaining backfires. Friendly works. (No, really.)
4. **Combo deals.** Ask the person you're renting from if they own the guesthouse you're staying at—bundle the rental into your accommodation deal.
5. **Off-season timing.** May–July and November–December see fewer tourists. Rental places are more flexible with pricing.

**Honest take from experience:** Record low was a Scoopy at $55/month in Sanur. In Canggu, I've never seen below $80. Location matters hugely.

## What documents you need

### For rental (just show these)
- **Passport** (any expiring date works)
- **Cash or QRIS** (mobile payment via QR code)

### For legal riding (carry always)
- **International Driving Permit (IDP)** + your home country's driver's license
- **Valid motorcycle category (A)** on your home license

Most travelers skip the IDP. **Don't.** Police checkpoints are real, and they care. An IDP costs $15–$30 from your country's auto association; fines run $15–$30 per stop. Do the math.

**For US, UK, EU, Canada, Australia citizens:** Get your IDP before you land. You'll need it. Indonesian police recognize them. Home country licenses alone won't cut it legally.

## Insurance—should you get it?

Most scooters rent without insurance. Full coverage runs an extra 30–50% of your daily rate.

**Get it if:**
- You're renting an NMAX or higher (replacement cost is real)
- You've never ridden a motorbike
- You're nervous about traffic

**Skip it if:**
- You're taking a Scoopy or Vario (cheap to replace parts)
- You're comfortable in chaos
- You're staying under two weeks (risk is low)

**Reality check:** Even with "full coverage," claims are tedious. Most locals just negotiate damage payments on the spot. Insurance policies here are looser than home. Still, for expensive bikes, it's worth the peace of mind.

## Police, traffic stops, and what to expect

Police set up checkpoints weekly. Honestly? They're looking for quick checks, not trouble. You'll know one's coming—usually a roadblock with orange cones.

**If stopped and you're clean:**
- Helmet on ✓
- IDP in your pocket ✓
- Polite demeanor ✓
→ They'll wave you through or ask one question and let you go.

**If you're missing the IDP:**
- They'll ask for it.
- You'll admit you don't have it.
- They'll hint at a fine (usually $15–$30).
- You settle on-the-spot in cash. (Officially, fines are higher, but "settle locally" is how it works.)

**What to do:**
- Stay calm. Don't argue.
- Smile. Use the phrase "Saya punya SIM internasional" (I have an international license) even if you don't—buys you credibility.
- If pushed, ask "Bisa bayar resmi?" (Can I pay officially?) They'll either let you go or agree to the informal settlement.

## Safety and riding rules

**Left-hand traffic.** Three days of weird, then muscle memory kicks in. Scooters weave between cars—that's normal, not reckless.

**Non-negotiables:**
- Helmet on, always
- City speed: max 40 km/h (25 mph)—no one follows this, but it exists
- Be predictable—no sudden swerves
- Watch for potholes, dogs, and sand on curves

**Before you ride:**
- Inspect brakes, lights, tread
- Take a photo/video of the bike's condition
- Test the throttle and clutch (if manual)
- Ask about the horn—yes, seriously; you'll use it constantly

## Common rookie mistakes

1. **No helmet.** Cops target this. Wear it even in heavy traffic—especially then.
2. **Leaving the bike unattended.** Theft happens. Lock it, even for coffee.
3. **Riding after dark without lights checked.** Bali's roads are dark. Broken headlight = police magnet + accident risk.
4. **Trusting the speedometer.** Most bikes are mechanically sound, but odometers lie. Don't rely on fuel gauge—gas up when it hits half-tank.
5. **Riding defensively into chaos.** Bali traffic is lawless. Assume every car might turn left suddenly. Ride paranoid.
6. **Not negotiating damage.** If you clip a mirror or scrape the fairing, tell the rental shop immediately with photos. Honesty = smaller repair costs.

A scooter isn't just transport on Bali—it's freedom. You're not beholden to taxi drivers, tour schedules, or ride-share prices. You can chase a sunset, find that empty beach, eat lunch where locals eat, and leave whenever you want. Yeah, the traffic feels scary for 48 hours. Yeah, you'll stall at a red light. ## FAQ

**Can I ride with a child?**
Technically, no—Indonesian law says no passengers under 9. Reality is looser, but a child + scooter on chaotic roads = bad idea anyway.

**Do I need a license to *rent*?**
No. To *ride legally*? Yes. IDP + home license, both.

**How do I pay?**
Cash (IDR) is standard. QRIS (local mobile payment via QR code) works at bigger shops. Cards rarely get accepted offline.

**Is insurance required?**
No, but recommended for expensive bikes or nervous riders.

**What if I crash?**
Call emergency (118 or 112). Tell your rental shop immediately. Negotiate damage on-site. Most shops are flexible if you're honest.

**What's the best bike for a beginner?**
Honda Scoopy or Vario 125. Both are forgiving, affordable, and light.

**Where's the cheapest gas?**
Pertamina stations (orange/red signs) are the national chain—cheaper and more reliable than roadside vendors.
