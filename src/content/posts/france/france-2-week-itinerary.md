## The Route (and Why We're Not Sprinting)

Here's the honest version: two weeks in France feels short until you start hopscotching between seven different cities and arrive at each one exhausted. This itinerary condenses you to **four bases** — Paris, Lyon, Provence (one village base), and Nice — so you actually *feel* the places instead of just photographing train stations.

**The route:** Paris (3 nights) → Lyon (3 nights) → Provence: Avignon or a Luberon village (4 nights with a rental car) → Côte d'Azur: Nice (3 nights) → fly home from Nice.

That's it. Four hotels. Three TGV journeys. Real time in each place. No 6 AM checkout panic, no "wait, where are we today?" confusion. My take: this is the difference between a trip you remember and a trip that's just a blur of hotel lobbies.

## Days 1–3: Paris (The Essential Dose)

Honestly, my first day in Paris was a jet-lagged blur. I wandered from my hotel near République down to the Marais, got lost three times, ate a €14 sandwich that somehow tasted like Paris anyway, and called it a win. This is fine. You're acclimating.

**Where to stay:** somewhere walkable but not Notre-Dame-adjacent (the prices punish you). I'd actually base myself in the **11th or 12th arrondissement** (République, Oberkampf, or Bastille neighborhoods) or the **Marais** proper. Not museum-famous, but full of people and cafés. Search [Booking.com for Paris](https://www.booking.com/searchresults.html?ss=Paris) and filter for 3-star/boutique hotels in the €80–140/night range — you'll find solid places.

**Day 1:** Arrive, settle in, walk around your neighborhood. Grab dinner somewhere local (not a tourist trap). An early night isn't failure; jet lag is real.

**Day 2:** Louvre or Musée d'Orsay in the morning (book skip-the-line tickets online, €19–22). Afternoon, walk to Sainte-Chapelle or Notre-Dame (just outside; the cathedral itself is under restoration through 2026, but the exterior is still photographable). Evening, find a wine bar in your neighborhood and sit for two hours like you live there.

**Day 3:** Montmartre in the morning (Sacré-Cœur, the steps, cheap sketches if that's your thing). Afternoon, either do Versailles as a day trip (trains from Gare Saint-Lazare, €8–12 return) or stay in the city and wander the Canal Saint-Martin. Pick up some cheese and wine for the train tomorrow.

**Booking:** Stay booked on [Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/searchresults.html?ss=Paris) — you'll find boutique options with good reviews for €100–130/night. Look for places with a metro connection (blue signs, very close to almost everything).

## Days 4–6: Lyon (Food and a Slower Gear)

The TGV from Paris Gare de Lyon to Lyon Part-Dieu takes 2 hours. I've done this leg a dozen times; book it 6–8 weeks early and you'll pay €25–40. Wait until a week before and you're looking at €70+.

Lyon is not Paris. It's quieter, richer (gastronomically), and full of locals instead of tourists. I love it for exactly this reason.

**Where to stay:** stay in **Vieux Lyon** (the old town, traboules everywhere — those hidden passageways between buildings) or **Presqu'île** near Place Bellecour. [Booking.com has options](https://www.booking.com/searchresults.html?ss=Lyon) for €70–110/night. Vieux Lyon feels more romantic; Presqu'île is more central.

**Day 4:** Arrive in the afternoon, get to your hotel, walk along the Saône. Don't over-schedule. Grab dinner at a traditional bouchon restaurant (casual, family-run, famous for Lyonnais cuisine like quenelles and saucisson). Budget €25–40 per person, no fancy reservations usually needed.

**Day 5:** Musée de la Confluence in the morning (modern, weird, free entry to most sections). Afternoon, read my [Lyon food guide](/france/lyon-food-guide/) — there's a reason this city punches above its weight culinarily. Visit Paul Bocuse market, pick up lunch, eat on a bench by the river. Evening, take the funicular up to the Basilica of Notre-Dame de Fourvière (it's kitsch and beautiful simultaneously).

**Day 6:** Rent a car here if you want to skip-test Provence roads before committing to 4 nights. Or just stay local: visit the Lumière brothers' museum (they invented cinema here), walk to Parc de la Tête d'Or (giant urban lake, totally worth an hour), grab your final dinner in a quieter neighborhood away from the tourists.

Quick tip: Lyon's metro is €1.70/ride or €10 for a 10-journey carnet. It's tiny and efficient — get a carnet.

## Days 7–10: Provence (Lavender, Villages, and a Rental Car)

This is where you actually *need* a car. The lavender fields, the perched villages (Gordes, Roussillon, Bonnieux), the Pont du Gard — they're all 20–45 minutes from any train station, scattered across the Vaucluse.

**The TGV leg:** Lyon to Avignon is 1 hour 10 minutes. Book 2 months early, €20–35. Book last-minute, €60+. I'd base myself in **Avignon proper** (easier to rent a car, bigger train station) or in a **Luberon village** like Lourmarin or Ménerbes if you want that postcard-perfect stone-village vibe.

**Where to stay:** 

- **Avignon (practical):** [Booking.com options](https://www.booking.com/searchresults.html?ss=Avignon) around €85–120/night in the center. You're 15 minutes from Pont du Gard, 45 from Gordes.
- **Lourmarin or Ménerbes (romantic):** €100–150/night but you're living *in* a village. No car = you're stuck. With a car = magic.

Car rental from Avignon's train station: €40–65/day for a small Renault or Peugeot. Book through [Booking.com's car rental widget](https://www.booking.com/searchresults.html?ss=Provence) or Rentalcars.com a few days ahead.

**Day 7:** Arrive Avignon, pick up your car, drive 15 minutes north to Pont du Gard (€10 entry). It's a Roman aqueduct, 2000 years old, three tiers of arches, insanely photogenic. Spend an hour, swim in the river if it's July. Drive back to your base for dinner.

**Day 8:** This is lavender + villages day. If you're visiting late June through early July, drive to the lavender fields around **Sault** or **Valensole** (both 45 minutes east from Lourmarin, longer from Avignon). Park near a field, walk in, take the photos everyone takes, and don't pick the flowers (they're monitored). Afternoon, drive through Gordes or Roussillon — these are the Instagram villages, genuinely beautiful, get there by 4 PM to beat the tour buses.

**Day 9:** Antibes is not Provence, but it's a short drive south from here and worth one night. Real take: it's a walled medieval town on the coast that *doesn't* scream "tourist trap" the way Nice does. Drive down (~1.5 hours from Lourmarin), drop your car, spend the night. Or if you're Provence-committed, stay put and do a day trip to Mont Ventoux (if you're a cyclist or like driving mountain roads) or revisit a village you loved.

**Day 10:** Return your car to Avignon (or Nice, if you're doing a one-way), and board the TGV to Nice. Avignon to Nice is 2 hours 30 minutes, €30–55 depending on how early you booked.

## Days 11–13: Côte d'Azur (Nice as Your Base)

You don't need a car on the Côte d'Azur. The coastal trains (TER/SNCF) run Nice–Antibes–Cannes–Menton every 15 minutes, €5–12 per journey. This is the opposite of Provence: it's about walkability and trains, not driving through lavender.

**Where to stay:** [Booking.com Nice](https://www.booking.com/searchresults.html?ss=Nice) has everything from €60–150/night depending on neighborhood. I'd stay in the **Vieux Nice** (old town, narrow streets, markets, sunset from the seafront) or **Negresco/Promenade des Anglais** area (famous, beachy, pricier). My take: Vieux Nice is grittier and more real; Negresco is postcard-perfect.

**Day 11:** Arrive Nice mid-afternoon, settle in. Walk the Promenade des Anglais, grab dinner by the beach. No big plans — you've earned a slow evening.

**Day 12:** Split this day: morning in Antibes (20-minute train ride, €5), afternoon back in Nice. Antibes has a smaller, less overwhelming vibe than Nice — walk the old town, visit the Picasso Museum if you want, sit for lunch on the harbor. Late afternoon, train back to Nice, dinner wherever you find yourself.

**Day 13:** You have flexibility here. Either do Cannes (30 minutes by train, famous, touristy, but beautiful), or pick one spot and *stay put*. Real take: I usually pick the latter on day 13 because I'm tired and a good meal + a long walk > the pressure of one more town. Grab lunch at a provençal restaurant, walk to Castle Hill (Château), watch the sunset over the entire coastline.

## The TGV: Booking Strategy and Budget

The French high-speed train is reliable, clean, and shockingly affordable if you book ahead. Here's how not to overpay:

**SNCF vs. Ouigo:**
- **SNCF** (main operator): €25–70 depending on route and timing. Book via [SNCF Connect app](https://www.sncf-connect.com/).
- **Ouigo** (budget SNCF subsidiary): €15–45, sometimes cheaper, but departs from secondary stations (Marne-la-Vallée outside Paris instead of Gare de Lyon, Bourg-Saint-Maurice for ski routes). Check if the station works for you.

**Booking timeline:**
- 10–12 weeks out: cheapest fares, €20–35 for Paris→Lyon, €15–25 for Lyon→Avignon.
- 6–8 weeks out: still good, €30–45.
- 2 weeks out: painful, €50–75.
- Last-minute (1 week): avoid unless desperate.

**My booking hack:** Set up SNCF alerts 12 weeks before your trip. When the €25 fares drop for your dates, book immediately. Don't overthink it.

**Real example (spring 2026 pricing):**
- Paris→Lyon TGV: €35 per person (2h)
- Lyon→Avignon TGV: €28 per person (1h10)
- Avignon→Nice TGV: €42 per person (2h30)
- Nice→Paris flight: €80–140 depending on airline and booking window

Total trains: ~€210 per person for all three TGVs. Flights home are separate.

## Budget for Two Weeks (Per Person, Mid-Range)

**Accommodation (€500–700):**
- Paris 3 nights: €100/night = €300
- Lyon 3 nights: €85/night = €255
- Provence 4 nights: €95/night = €380
- Nice 3 nights: €100/night = €300
- **Subtotal: ~€1,235**

Okay, that's pricier than I said. Adjust down to €70–80/night and you're at €900–1,000.

**Food (€400–550):**
- Paris: €60/day (mix of €12 sandwiches, €30 dinners, café breakfasts)
- Lyon: €55/day (markets are cheap, bouchon dinners €35–45)
- Provence: €45/day (picnics from markets, one nice dinner)
- Nice: €50/day (casual seafood, touristy markup)
- **Subtotal: €700–800**

Reality check: if you picnic 50% of lunches and pick 2–3 budget dinners instead of fancy ones, you're at €400–450.

**Trains (€210–280):**
- Three TGVs: €210 (if booked early)
- Metro/local trains: ~€30

**Car rental (€200–280, only if doing Provence alone):**
- 4 days, €50/day = €200
- Gas: ~€30

**Activities (€100–150):**
- Museum entries, cable cars, guides — budget light here

**Total per person (2 weeks): €1,600–2,100**

## My Honest Take

Here's the vulnerable part: the first time I did two weeks in France, I booked 8 cities in 14 days. I saw everything. I remember almost nothing except being tired and my feet hurting. The second time, I did this exact route — 4 bases, slower pace — and I came home *full* instead of empty. I could actually tell you about the bouchon restaurant owner's dog, the lavender field where I sat for two hours reading, the sunset in Nice that made me cry.

Two weeks is not a long time. If you pack it tightly, you'll move fast and see a lot. But there's a cost: you'll miss the moments that make travel actually *matter*. This itinerary is built for the moments instead — fewer hotel changes, more nights in each place, real time to wander and sit and eat.

The TGV makes this pace possible. Those trains are fast enough that you're never stuck, but not so fast that you're disconnected. You board in Paris with croissants, sleep a bit, and you're in Lyon. That's the magic of France at a human speed.
