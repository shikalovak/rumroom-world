## The Route (and Why We're Not Sprinting)

Two weeks in France feels short until you start hopscotching between seven different cities and arrive at each one exhausted. This itinerary condenses the trip to **four bases** — Paris, Lyon, Provence (one village base), and Nice — so you actually *feel* the places instead of just photographing train stations.

**The route:** Paris (3 nights) → Lyon (3 nights) → Provence: Avignon or a Luberon village (4 nights with a rental car) → Côte d'Azur: Nice (3 nights) → fly home from Nice.

That's it. Four hotels. Three TGV journeys. Real time in each place. No 6 AM checkout panic, no scrambling to remember which city you're in. This structure tends to make for a trip you remember, rather than a blur of hotel lobbies.

## Days 1–3: Paris (The Essential Dose)

A jet-lagged first day in Paris is normal — expect to wander, get turned around a couple of times, grab something to eat that tastes better than it should, and call it a win. You're acclimating.

**Where to stay:** somewhere walkable but not immediately next to Notre-Dame (the prices punish you for it). The **11th or 12th arrondissement** (République, Oberkampf, or Bastille neighborhoods) or the **Marais** proper are good picks — not museum-famous, but full of people and cafés. Search [Booking.com for Paris](https://www.booking.com/searchresults.html?ss=Paris) and filter for 3-star/boutique hotels in the €80–140/night range.

**Day 1:** Arrive, settle in, walk around your neighborhood. Grab dinner somewhere local (not a tourist trap). An early night isn't failure; jet lag is real.

**Day 2:** Louvre or Musée d'Orsay in the morning (book skip-the-line tickets online, roughly €19–22 — check current prices before booking, as museum ticket costs are adjusted periodically). Afternoon, walk to Sainte-Chapelle or Notre-Dame (the cathedral reopened to visitors in December 2024 after restoration, so check current visiting hours). Evening, find a wine bar in your neighborhood and sit for a couple of hours.

**Day 3:** Montmartre in the morning (Sacré-Cœur, the steps, street artists if that's your thing). Afternoon, either do Versailles as a day trip (trains from Gare Saint-Lazare or RER C, roughly €8–12 return) or stay in the city and wander the Canal Saint-Martin. Pick up some cheese and wine for the train tomorrow.

**Booking:** [Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/searchresults.html?ss=Paris) tends to have boutique options with good reviews for €100–130/night. Look for places with a nearby metro connection.

## Days 4–6: Lyon (Food and a Slower Gear)

The TGV from Paris Gare de Lyon to Lyon Part-Dieu takes about 2 hours. Book 6–8 weeks early and expect roughly €25–40; wait until a week before and fares can climb past €70.

Lyon is not Paris — it's quieter, richer gastronomically, and full of locals rather than tourists.

**Where to stay:** **Vieux Lyon** (the old town, traboules everywhere — hidden passageways between buildings) or **Presqu'île** near Place Bellecour. [Booking.com has options](https://www.booking.com/searchresults.html?ss=Lyon) for roughly €70–110/night. Vieux Lyon feels more romantic; Presqu'île is more central.

**Day 4:** Arrive in the afternoon, get to your hotel, walk along the Saône. Don't over-schedule. Grab dinner at a traditional bouchon (casual, family-run, famous for Lyonnais dishes like quenelles and saucisson). Budget roughly €25–40 per person; reservations usually aren't essential for a weekday dinner but are worth making on weekends.

**Day 5:** Musée des Confluences in the morning (modern, unusual architecture, some sections free to enter). Afternoon, check the [Lyon food guide](/france/lyon-food-guide/) for where to eat — there's a reason this city punches above its weight culinarily. Visit Les Halles Paul Bocuse market, pick up lunch, eat on a bench by the river. Evening, take the funicular up to the Basilica of Notre-Dame de Fourvière.

**Day 6:** Rent a car here if you want to test Provence roads before committing to 4 nights. Or stay local: visit the Lumière brothers' museum (widely credited with early cinema history), walk to Parc de la Tête d'Or (a large urban park with a lake), and grab a final dinner in a quieter neighborhood away from the busiest tourist streets.

Quick tip: Lyon's metro/tram network runs on a per-ride or multi-ride carnet basis — check current fares on the TCL app, as prices are adjusted periodically. It's compact and efficient.

## Days 7–10: Provence (Lavender, Villages, and a Rental Car)

This is where a car genuinely helps. The lavender fields, the perched villages (Gordes, Roussillon, Bonnieux), and the Pont du Gard are all 20–45 minutes from any train station, scattered across the Vaucluse.

**The TGV leg:** Lyon to Avignon is about 1 hour 10 minutes. Book 2 months early and expect roughly €20–35; last-minute fares can run €60+. **Avignon proper** is a practical base (easier to rent a car, bigger train station), or a **Luberon village** like Lourmarin or Ménerbes if you want a postcard-perfect stone-village stay.

**Where to stay:**

- **Avignon (practical):** [Booking.com options](https://www.booking.com/searchresults.html?ss=Avignon) around €85–120/night in the center. You're roughly 15 minutes from Pont du Gard, 45 from Gordes.
- **Lourmarin or Ménerbes (romantic):** €100–150/night, but you're living *in* a village. Without a car you're fairly stuck; with one, it works well.

Car rental from Avignon's train station: roughly €40–65/day for a small hatchback. Book through [Booking.com's car rental widget](https://www.booking.com/searchresults.html?ss=Provence) or a rental aggregator a few days ahead.

**Day 7:** Arrive Avignon, pick up your car, drive about 15 minutes north to Pont du Gard (entry fees apply and vary by season — check current pricing before you go). It's a Roman aqueduct roughly 2,000 years old, three tiers of arches, genuinely photogenic. Spend an hour, swim in the river if conditions allow. Drive back to your base for dinner.

**Day 8:** Lavender-and-villages day. If you're visiting late June through early July, drive to the lavender fields around **Sault** or **Valensole** (roughly 45 minutes east from Lourmarin, longer from Avignon). Park near a field, walk in, and don't pick the flowers — many fields are actively farmed and monitored. Afternoon, drive through Gordes or Roussillon — get there by mid-afternoon to beat the tour buses.

**Day 9:** Antibes is not Provence, but it's a short drive south and worth a night if your route allows it. It's a walled medieval town on the coast with a notably different feel from Nice. Drive down (roughly 1.5 hours from Lourmarin), drop your car, spend the night. Or, if you'd rather stay Provence-based, do a day trip to Mont Ventoux (popular with cyclists and drivers who enjoy mountain roads) or revisit a village you liked.

**Day 10:** Return your car to Avignon (or Nice, if doing a one-way rental — confirm one-way fees, which are often substantial), and board the TGV to Nice. Avignon to Nice is roughly 2 hours 30 minutes, €30–55 depending on how early you booked.

## Days 11–13: Côte d'Azur (Nice as Your Base)

You don't need a car on the Côte d'Azur. The coastal TER/SNCF trains run Nice–Antibes–Cannes–Menton frequently, roughly €5–12 per journey. This stretch is about walkability and trains, not driving through lavender.

**Where to stay:** [Booking.com Nice](https://www.booking.com/searchresults.html?ss=Nice) has options from roughly €60–150/night depending on neighborhood. **Vieux Nice** (old town, narrow streets, markets, sunset from the seafront) tends to feel grittier and more local; the **Promenade des Anglais** area is more polished and beach-forward.

**Day 11:** Arrive Nice mid-afternoon, settle in. Walk the Promenade des Anglais, grab dinner by the beach. No big plans for the first evening.

**Day 12:** Split the day: morning in Antibes (roughly a 20-minute train ride), afternoon back in Nice. Antibes has a smaller, less overwhelming feel than Nice — walk the old town, visit the Picasso Museum if you're interested, sit for lunch on the harbor. Late afternoon, train back to Nice for dinner.

**Day 13:** Flexible day. Either do Cannes (roughly 30 minutes by train, touristy but scenic), or pick one spot in Nice and stay put — a good meal and a long walk can be a better use of a last day than squeezing in one more town. Consider lunch at a Provençal restaurant and a walk to Castle Hill (Colline du Château) for sunset views over the coastline.

## The TGV: Booking Strategy and Budget

The French high-speed train network is generally reliable and affordable if you book ahead. Here's how to avoid overpaying:

**SNCF vs. Ouigo:**
- **SNCF** (main operator): roughly €25–70 depending on route and timing. Book via [SNCF Connect](https://www.sncf-connect.com/).
- **Ouigo** (budget SNCF subsidiary): roughly €15–45, sometimes cheaper, but departs from secondary stations (Marne-la-Vallée outside Paris instead of Gare de Lyon, for example). Check whether the station works for your plans.

**Booking timeline (general guidance — actual fares vary by route, season, and demand):**
- 10–12 weeks out: typically the cheapest fares available.
- 6–8 weeks out: still generally good value.
- 2 weeks out: noticeably pricier.
- Last-minute (1 week): often the most expensive option.

**Booking tip:** Set up SNCF fare alerts a few months before your trip so you can book as soon as low fares for your dates appear.

**Illustrative example pricing (subject to change — always check current fares before booking):**
- Paris→Lyon TGV: roughly €35 per person (2h) if booked well ahead
- Lyon→Avignon TGV: roughly €28 per person (1h10)
- Avignon→Nice TGV: roughly €42 per person (2h30)
- Nice→Paris flight: roughly €80–140 depending on airline and booking window

Total trains: roughly €200–220 per person for all three TGVs if booked early. Flights home are separate.

## Budget for Two Weeks (Per Person, Mid-Range)

**Accommodation (roughly €900–1,235, depending on how you book):**
- Paris 3 nights: €100/night = €300
- Lyon 3 nights: €85/night = €255
- Provence 4 nights: €95/night = €380
- Nice 3 nights: €100/night = €300
- **Subtotal at these rates: ~€1,235**

Booking slightly cheaper places (€70–80/night average) brings the same itinerary down to roughly €900–1,000.

**Food (roughly €400–800, depending on how much you picnic vs. dine out):**
- Paris: roughly €60/day (mix of quick lunches, sit-down dinners, café breakfasts)
- Lyon: roughly €55/day (markets are cheap, bouchon dinners run higher)
- Provence: roughly €45/day (picnics from markets, one nicer dinner)
- Nice: roughly €50/day (casual seafood, some touristy markup)

Picnicking for half your lunches and choosing a couple of budget dinners over fancier ones brings this down toward €400–450 total; eating out more often pushes it toward €700–800.

**Trains (roughly €210–280):**
- Three TGVs: roughly €200–220 if booked early
- Metro/local trains: roughly €30

**Car rental (roughly €200–280, only if doing Provence with a car):**
- 4 days at roughly €50/day: €200
- Fuel: roughly €30

**Activities (roughly €100–150):**
- Museum entries, cable cars, guides

**Total per person (2 weeks): roughly €1,600–2,100**, depending on how you balance accommodation and food choices.

## The Bottom Line

Two weeks is not a long time. Packing it with eight cities in fourteen days means moving fast and seeing a lot, but at a cost: you'll likely spend more time tired and in transit than actually settling into any one place. This itinerary trades city count for pace — fewer hotel changes, more nights in each spot, real time to wander, eat, and sit still for a while.

The TGV network makes this kind of pace possible. The trains are fast enough that you're never stuck for long, but not so fast that the country blurs by — board in Paris, and a couple of hours later you're in Lyon.
